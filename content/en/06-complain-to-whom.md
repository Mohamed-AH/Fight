# 6. Complain to whom?

> "Complain to a friend and you grieve him, or to an enemy and he gloats." A wise man, quoted by Dr. Saad Al-Arifi ([32:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MSsimWznk3U&t=1970s)) <!-- F071 F072 -->

## From the episode

- **Two kinds of complaint.** To people, and to God. <!-- F066 -->
- **Complaining to people.** Some tell everyone, in every gathering, even the stranger in a clinic waiting room. He says this does not relieve the pain, it renews it. <!-- F067 --> Each time the wound tries to close, you open it again. <!-- F068 -->
- **Complaining to God.** The prophets complained only to God: Jacob's "I only complain of my grief and sorrow to God", and the Prophet's prayer "O God, to You I complain of my weakness and lack of means". <!-- F069 F070 -->
- **The wise man.** He followed a man who had been complaining to a friend and told him: complain only to God; a friend will be grieved and an enemy will gloat. "I have not seen with this eye for 15 years, and I have told no one." <!-- F071 F072 F073 -->
- **The third kind: venting.** Venting is allowed, but the real question is to whom. <!-- F074 -->
- **Two qualities.** The person must have sound judgment and keep secrets: they will not repeat what you said, and they will give advice that helps you out. <!-- F075 -->
- **Rare.** Out of 50 or 100 friends, perhaps one or two qualify. <!-- F076 -->

## The idea, explained

<!-- explained -->

### Why telling everyone can make it worse

Talking about a problem over and over, without moving towards a solution, has a name in psychology: **co-rumination**. It brings people closer, but it also raises anxiety and low mood ([Rose, 2002](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-8624.00509)). Venting anger in the hope of "getting it out" tends to keep it going rather than drain it ([Bushman, 2002](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167202289002)). That is his image of the wound reopened every time it starts to close.

### Why the right person helps

Research on the social sharing of emotions finds that simply re-telling a painful event does not help much on its own; what helps is when the listener helps you see it differently or find a way forward ([Rimé, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1177/1754073908097189)). His two conditions fit this closely: **sound judgment** (someone who can help you reframe or act) and **discretion** (someone safe to tell).

| Who you tell | What usually happens |
| --- | --- |
| Everyone, often | The pain is renewed; some listeners gloat |
| A worried friend | You share the weight, and he carries it too |
| Someone wise and discreet | You get a new angle and a next step |
| God | In his view, the complaint that does not wound |
