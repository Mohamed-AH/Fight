# 12. Practical steps through a crisis

> "The world gives in order to take... an event took part of you and left most of you." A visitor to a grieving king, quoted by Dr. Saad Al-Arifi ([1:22:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MSsimWznk3U&t=4924s)) <!-- F155 F156 -->

## From the episode

- **Don't seat the worry.** Some people bring a chair into the middle of their mind, seat the worry on it, and serve it coffee and tea. Don't let negative feelings spread; the longer you dwell, the deeper the roots. <!-- F150 F151 -->
- **Leave panic and complaint.** They deepen the pain. <!-- F152 -->
- **Don't withdraw.** Meeting people breaks the ring of the crisis; alone, you only talk to yourself about the worry. <!-- F153 -->
- **Count what remains.** Focus on what you have, not on what was taken. <!-- F154 --> An ancient Himyarite king withdrew from everyone after his heir was killed by a horse, until a visitor told him: "the world gives in order to take... an event took part of you and left most of you." <!-- F155 F156 -->
- **The security guard.** Leaving a government office in despair over a stuck application, he grumbled to a guard. The guard said: if everything else in your life is working, thank God for that blessing. <!-- F157 -->
- **Those worse off.** A friend in Tabuk, eldest of nine, lost his parents and all his siblings in one car accident. "Yesterday you had a big family; today it is zero." <!-- F158 -->
- **Keep life's circles apart.** Work, friends, health, hobbies, free time. <!-- F159 --> Don't carry a work crisis home and take it out on your family. <!-- F160 -->
- **Look after yourself.** Shave, dress well; buy something you like. <!-- F161 --> Al-Ahnaf ibn Qays told a man clutching a dirham: it will not benefit you until you part with it. <!-- F162 -->
- **Don't bottle up grief.** Vent, but to someone with judgment who keeps secrets. <!-- F163 --> Not like the friend whose only advice after a 14-minute call was "sort yourself out". <!-- F164 -->
- **Accept failure.** It is common to most people; failing once is not failing in life; and failure shows you are brave enough to try. <!-- F165 F166 F167 -->
- **Sleep and exercise.** Enough sleep and exercise matter. <!-- F168 -->
- **Review how you think.** Excessive sensitivity is sometimes the crisis itself. <!-- F169 -->
- **Say sorry first.** To end a dispute, don't ask who was wrong; ask whether you deserve peace. "If I was wrong, I'm sorry; if you were, I forgive you." <!-- F170 -->
- **Your phone's wallpaper.** Put an optimistic phrase there instead of a waterfall. <!-- F171 -->
- **Stop asking "why me?"** It is one of the things that most prolongs a crisis. <!-- F172 -->
- **Imagine without.** Imagine life without health, then family, then friends, then work. Now take them all back. <!-- F173 -->
- **Do something kind.** Let the driver into your lane, hold the door. <!-- F174 --> A friend keeps 5-riyal notes in the car for the petrol attendant or the delivery boy. <!-- F175 --> "Among the best deeds is joy you bring into a Muslim's heart." <!-- F176 -->
- **Treat yourself as a friend.** If a friend called in crisis you would say "it's simple, be patient, it will pass". Say the same to yourself. <!-- F177 -->
- **It is temporary.** Believe the crisis will pass. <!-- F178 -->

## Career setbacks and unemployment

[Watch from 1:44:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MSsimWznk3U&t=6276s)

- **The hardest professional crisis.** The host asks about unemployment after many attempts. <!-- F179 -->
- **Life is not tailored to us.** Sometimes you don't get option one; you get option two, and the good is in option two, as Tabuk was for him. <!-- F180 F181 -->
- **Not numbness.** He is not asking people to numb themselves, but to keep striving within what is available. <!-- F182 -->
- **Complete from now.** Treat your life as complete from now, and keep working to complete it. <!-- F183 -->
