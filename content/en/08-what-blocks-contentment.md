# 8. What blocks contentment

> "Comparison is the door to misery." Dr. Saad Al-Arifi ([47:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MSsimWznk3U&t=2830s)) <!-- F096 -->

## From the episode

- **Comparison.** The first and worst cause. We compare with the wrong person, in the wrong way, at the wrong time: when we have just fallen. <!-- F096 F097 -->
- **The villa.** Rent is due and you cannot pay it, and your colleague owns a villa. But did you compare everything? Your health may be excellent and his poor; your children well and his in hospital. A fair comparison covers all of life, not only what you lack. <!-- F098 F099 -->
- **Gifts, not rights.** When God takes something you are angry, because you think it was yours by right. Blessings are gifts. <!-- F100 -->
- **Expecting perfection.** A friend needed 10,000 riyals and went to the brother he was sure would send twenty. The brother said: sorry, I can't. Do not expect perfection, even from those closest to you. <!-- F101 F102 -->
- **Bad company.** Sitting with the discontented and constant complainers, who talk only of disease, disasters and wars, poisons the mind. <!-- F103 -->
- **Not looking at those worse off.** A friend with a full house was crushed by his son's illness. The day before, another friend had visited: his three sons had each weakened from about the age of ten and died at 13 or 14. "Praise God that He took one thing and left you many." <!-- F104 F105 -->
- **"Social disconnection" media.** He prefers to call social media *disconnection* media: four friends in a café, each on his phone. Seeing others' travel and cars while you struggle with rent breeds discontent. <!-- F106 -->
- **Believing it will last.** A hard time feels permanent, but circumstances change. There is always light at the end of the tunnel; if you can't see it, there may be a bend before it. <!-- F107 F108 -->
- **The diagnosis that changed.** A friend's wife was told she needed surgery and 15 days in hospital just before the children's exams. He fell into deep gloom. That day he gave money to a struggling employee, with the intention of her recovery. Before the man had closed the door, the hospital called: a new diagnosis, no surgery needed. <!-- F109 -->
- **Harsh self-talk.** Sometimes your fiercest companion is yourself. <!-- F110 --> "Wherever I go the doors close, I'm a failure": what you say to yourself does as much harm as what others say to you, and in his view life responds to it. <!-- F111 F112 -->
