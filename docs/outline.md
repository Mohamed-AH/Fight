# Outline: the crisis episode (`fight`)

Status: **draft, waiting for the owner's approval.**

## The episode

- **Petroly podcast** (بودكاست بترولي, Mukhtalif radio), host **Ahmed Attar**, guest **Dr. Saad Al-Arifi**, university professor and trainer in influence and persuasion. [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MSsimWznk3U). Same host as the Claiming Ideas episode.
- **The argument:** crises are built into life, so expect them instead of being ambushed by them. How you receive a crisis (discontent, patience, contentment or gratitude) decides how heavy it is and how long it lasts. Contentment (الرضا) can be learned: stop unfair comparisons, notice what you still have, talk to yourself like a friend, and take practical steps while trusting that the good in it will show itself later.
- The guest argues from an Islamic frame (Qur'an, hadith, stories of the Prophet and the prophets) and from many real stories, several of them his own.

## Signature interactive tool

**"My crisis kit"** (عُدّتي للأزمات): the practical steps from chapter 12, plus one item from each earlier chapter, as a checklist the reader builds for a crisis they are in (or one they want to be ready for). They pick the steps they will try, add their own three blessings, and print or save it. Stored only on their device. Every item links back to the chapter that explains it.

## The idea, explained: outside sources to check against

The site presents the guest's faith-based views as his views and adds evidence where he makes claims about the body or the mind:

- Stress, grief and the immune system (he says sadness "switches off" immunity): psychoneuroimmunology reviews, e.g. Segerstrom & Miller (2004) meta-analysis.
- The three-blessings exercise: Seligman et al. (2005), "Positive psychology progress", the "three good things" study.
- Hedonic adaptation (the waterfall): Brickman & Campbell; Lyubomirsky.
- Social comparison and social media: Festinger's social comparison theory; recent studies on passive social media use.
- Self-compassion ("treat yourself like a friend"): Kristin Neff.
- Counting the word "patience" in the Qur'an (he says about 90 times): check against a concordance.
- The quote he attributes to Carl Jung: attribution to verify; likely leave out if it cannot be sourced.
- Verses and hadith quoted: give the reference (surah and verse, hadith collection) for each.

## Care and tone

- Many stories are about death, illness and loss. Keep the real people unnamed, as he did.
- Add a short, gentle note on the home and practical-steps pages: if a crisis is too heavy to carry, talk to a doctor or a mental-health professional (the guest's advice is not a substitute).
- Marital "nature" (chapter 3) is presented as his view, briefly.
